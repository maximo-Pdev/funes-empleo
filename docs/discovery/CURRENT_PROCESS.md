# Current employment-office process

## Evidence quality

This process was reconstructed from the current municipal website, class presentations, and an automatically generated group-meeting transcript. Speaker attribution in the transcript is unreliable because many participants were recorded through two Meet audio sources. The sequence below captures the consistent business process, not verbatim statements.

## Current process — as is

1. A company communicates a staffing need by phone, WhatsApp, email, or direct contact with Employment Office staff.
2. Staff speak with the company to understand the position and requirements.
3. Publication often requires requesting a designed promotional piece and distributing it through municipal channels.
4. Candidate CVs arrive on paper, by email, through the current municipal web form, or in person.
5. Staff review the CV or interview the person and record information in Excel.
6. Candidate classification is limited and inconsistent. The current website appears to assign a single occupational category.
7. When an opening must be filled, staff manually search CVs and spreadsheet rows.
8. Staff contact promising candidates and conduct a municipal pre-interview.
9. The office refers a small selection of candidates to the company; the transcript suggests roughly five or six CVs per position, but the exact number is not a fixed rule.
10. The company interviews candidates and communicates an outcome when possible.
11. Staff record activity and results in the spreadsheet.
12. Candidates who are not selected return to the active pool and may be considered for another opening.
13. Some candidates receive employability training about interviews, presentation, communication, and workplace expectations.
14. Staff may follow hired candidates through WhatsApp or company visits.

## Current channels and artifacts

- Paper CVs.
- PDF CVs.
- Municipal employment web form.
- Excel spreadsheets.
- Email.
- WhatsApp.
- Phone calls.
- In-person interviews and company visits.
- Designed images or posts used to advertise openings.

## Main pain points

- Candidate information is fragmented across channels and formats.
- A single category can hide candidates who can perform several kinds of work.
- Search is manual and can take days.
- Old candidates become difficult to find as new CVs arrive.
- Publication and candidate handoff require manual coordination.
- Application and referral status is not visible in one place.
- Contact and follow-up history is difficult to reconstruct.
- Operational metrics require manual counting.
- Digital-only processes could exclude candidates who need assistance.

## Target process — to be validated by the specification

1. A company registers, signs in, and maintains its organization profile.
2. The company drafts a job opening and submits it for municipal review.
3. An administrator approves it, requests corrections, or rejects it.
4. An approved opening is published.
5. A candidate registers and completes a profile, or staff create an assisted profile in the office.
6. The candidate selects multiple categories and uploads a PDF CV.
7. The candidate applies to a published opening, or an administrator associates a suitable active candidate with it.
8. Administrators search and filter candidates, record a pre-interview, and decide whom to refer.
9. Only referred candidates become visible to the relevant company.
10. The company records or communicates the interview outcome.
11. Administrators confirm the final outcome and maintain the application history.
12. Candidates not selected may return to the active pool or be associated with training.
13. Dashboards and exports summarize operational activity.

## Core insight

This is a managed intermediation workflow, not merely a public job board. Search, preselection, referral, history, and follow-up are more important than visual publication alone.
